# TESLA VALUATION COMPARISON: TRADITIONAL VS. GROWTH-BASED APPROACH

## Summary of Valuation Results
Traditional Residual Earnings Valuation: $27.06 per share
Growth-Based DCF Valuation: $224.46 per share
Difference: $197.40 per share (729.5% higher)

## Main Factors Explaining the Difference

### 1. Discount Rate (WACC)
* Traditional approach uses a high WACC of 27.74%
* Growth-based approach uses a more reasonable WACC of 12.00%
* Lower WACC significantly increases present value of future cash flows

### 2. Future Growth Projections
* Traditional approach only uses historical residual earnings
* Growth-based approach explicitly models future revenue growth:
  - EV revenue: 25% → 5% growth over 10 years
  - Software/FSD: 40% → 10% growth over 10 years
  - Energy storage: 45% → 10% growth over 10 years
  - Robotaxi: New revenue stream starting in year 3

### 3. Improving Profit Margins
* Traditional approach uses historical operating margin (20.99% RNOA)
* Growth-based approach models improving margins by segment:
  - EV margins: 8% → 15%
  - Software margins: 70% → 80%
  - Energy margins: 12% → 25%
  - Robotaxi margins: 50% → 75%

### 4. Intangible Assets Valuation
* Traditional approach ignores intangible assets
* Growth-based approach quantifies intangible value:
  - Brand value: $29.3B
  - Technology/patents: $10.0B
  - Network effects: $0.1B
  - Leadership premium: $4.4B

### 5. Terminal Value Calculation
* Traditional approach has no explicit terminal value
* Growth-based approach uses Gordon Growth Model with 4% terminal growth rate
* Terminal value accounts for $418.5B of total value

## Conclusion
The significant difference between the two valuations demonstrates how accounting-based
valuation methods (like residual earnings) tend to undervalue high-growth, intangible-rich
companies like Tesla. The growth-based approach better reflects Tesla's future potential by:

1. Using a more appropriate discount rate
2. Explicitly modeling future growth across multiple segments
3. Accounting for improving margins from scale and product mix shift
4. Valuing Tesla's significant intangible assets
5. Including terminal value that captures long-term growth potential

This explains why Tesla's market valuation is much closer to the growth-based approach
than the traditional residual earnings valuation.
