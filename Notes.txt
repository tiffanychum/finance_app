## 1. Overview: The Five Steps of Fundamental Analysis

This provides a framework for the entire process:

1. **Knowing the Business:** Understanding products, technology, competition, management, and the regulatory environment. (Covered in Section 2)
2. **Analyzing Information:** Reviewing financial statements and other relevant data. (Covered in Section 3)
3. **Forecasting Payoffs:** Predicting future financial performance (e.g., earnings, cash flows). Key aspects include measuring value added and forecasting value added. (Partially covered in Section 5 & 6)
4. **Convert Forecasts to a Valuation:** Using models to estimate the firm's intrinsic value. (Covered in Sections 3, 4, 5)
5. **Trading on the Valuation:** Making investment decisions (BUY, SELL, HOLD) based on comparing the estimated value to the market price.

## 3. Financial Statement Analysis & Valuation Models (Steps 2, 4)

### 3.1. Core Concepts: Distinguishing Operations vs. Financing

It's crucial to separate activities related to the core business operations from financing activities.

* **Operating Activities:** Relate to the primary revenue-generating activities (selling goods/services). Key metrics include Operating Income (OI) and Net Operating Assets (NOA).
* **Financing Activities:** Relate to how the firm funds its operations (debt, equity). Key metrics include Net Financial Expense (NFE) and Net Financial Obligations (NFO).

### 3.2. Valuation using Residual Earnings (RE)

* **Residual Earnings (RE):** Measures the earnings generated above the required return for equity investors.
  ```
  RE_t = Comprehensive Earnings_t - (ρ_E - 1) * CSE_{t-1}
  ```
  Where:
  * `ρ_E` = Required return on common equity (Cost of Equity)
  * `CSE_{t-1}` = Common Shareholders' Equity at the beginning of the period (Book Value)
* **Valuation Formula:**
  ```
  V_0 = CSE_0 + PV of future RE
  ```
* **Components (from Screenshot 4):**
### Earnings Components and Corresponding Residual Earnings Measures

| Net Income Component | Book Value Component | Residual Earnings Measure |
|---------------------|----------------------|---------------------------|
| Operating Income (OI) | Net Operating Assets (NOA) | OIt – (ρF – 1) NOAt-1 (ReOI) |
| Net Financial Expense (NFE) | Net Financial Obligations (NFO) | NFEt – (ρD – 1) NFOt-1 (ReNFE) ~0 if measured in market value |
| Earnings | Common Stockholders' Equity (CSE) | Earnt – (ρE – 1) CSEt-1 (RE) |

* **Focus:**
  * Residual Operating Income (ReOI) is central to valuing operations.
  * Residual Net Financial Expense (ReNFE) is often close to zero if financial assets/liabilities are measured at market value.
  * When Price-to-Book (P/B) = 1, RE should be zero.

* **Steps in Applying the RE Model:**
  1. Identify the book value (CSE) in the most recent balance sheet.
  2. Forecast earnings and dividends up to a forecast horizon.
  3. Forecast future book values from current book values and forecasts of earnings and dividends.
  4. Calculate future residual earnings (RE or ReOI) from the forecasts.
  5. Discount the residual earnings to present value.
  6. Calculate a continuing value (CV) at the forecast horizon.
  7. Discount the continuing value to the present value.
  8. Value = Current Book Value + PV(RE) + PV(CV)

### 3.3. Valuation using Abnormal Earnings Growth (AEG)

* **Abnormal Earnings Growth (AEG):** Measures the growth in earnings above what's considered "normal" given the required return. Change in RE = AEG.
* AEG is the focus for Price-to-Earnings (P/E) valuation.
* **Valuation Formula:**
  ```
  V_0 = Earnings_1 / (ρ_E - 1) + PV of future AEG
  ```
  (Capitalized forward earnings + PV of Changes in RE)
Earnings Components and Corresponding Abnormal Earnings Growth Measures 
| Earnings Component          | Abnormal Earnings Growth Measure                             |
|-----------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| Operating income (OI)       | Abnormal operating income growth: ` [OI_t + (ρ_F - 1) * FCF_{t-1}] - ρ_F * OI_{t-1}` or `[G_t - ρ_F] * OI_{t-1}`   |
| Net financing expense (NFE) | Abnormal net financial expense growth: `[NFE_t + (ρ_D - 1) * F_{t-1}] - ρ_D * NFE_{t-1}`   |
| Earnings                    | Abnormal earnings growth: `[Earn_t + (ρ_E - 1) * d_{t-1}] - ρ_E * Earn_{t-1}` |

* Note: `FCF` = Free Cash Flow, `G` = Growth rate, `d` = dividends, `F` = Net Financial Assets/Liabilities.

## 4. The Analysis of Profitability: Decomposing ROCE (Step 2 Cont.)

Return on Common Equity (ROCE) is a key measure. We decompose it to understand its drivers.

**Overall Scheme**
Return on Common Equity is driven by:
1. Return from Operating Activities -> Return on Net Operating Assets (RNOA) -> Profit Margin (PM) & Asset Turnover (ATO) -> Gross Margin/Expense Drivers & Individual Asset/Liability Drivers
2. Return from Financing Activities -> Financial Leverage x Spread -> Net Borrowing Cost Drivers

### 4.1. Level 1: Operating vs. Financing Contribution

```
ROCE = RNOA + FLEV * SPREAD
```
Where:
* **RNOA (Return on Net Operating Assets):** Measures operating performance independent of financing. `RNOA = OI / NOA` (OI = Operating Income after tax, NOA = Net Operating Assets = OA - OL)
* **FLEV (Financial Leverage):** Measures the degree of debt financing. `FLEV = NFO / CSE` (NFO = Net Financial Obligations)
* **SPREAD:** Measures the difference between operating return and the cost of borrowing. `SPREAD = RNOA - NBC`
* **NBC (Net Borrowing Cost):** Effective after-tax cost of net debt. (Formula in Section 4.4)

> Leverage increases ROCE *only if* the SPREAD is positive (RNOA > NBC). This benefits shareholders.

### 4.2. Level 2: RNOA Decomposition & Operating Liability Leverage

* **RNOA decomposed into Margin and Turnover:**
  ```
  RNOA = PM * ATO
  ```
  * **PM (Operating Profit Margin):** `OI / Sales` - Profitability of sales.
  * **ATO (Asset Turnover):** `Sales / NOA` - Efficiency in using assets to generate sales.

* **Operating Liability Leverage (OLLEV):** RNOA can be further analyzed considering operating liabilities.
  ```
  RNOA = ROOA + (OLLEV * OLSPREAD)
  ```
  * **ROOA (Return on Operating Assets):** `(OI + Implicit Interest on OL (after tax)) / OA`
  * **OLLEV (Operating Liability Leverage):** `OL / NOA`
  * **OLSPREAD:** `ROOA - Short-term Borrowing Rate (after tax)`
  > Firms often benefit from operating liabilities (e.g., accounts payable) as the implicit cost (SBR) is low.

* **Putting it all together:**
  ```
  ROCE = ROOA + (OLLEV * OLSPREAD) + (FLEV * SPREAD)
  ```

### 4.3. Level 3: Profit Margin and Asset Turnover Drivers

* **Profit Margin Drivers (from Screenshot 1):**
  ```
  PM = Sales PM + Other Operating Income PM
  ```
  ```
  Sales PM = (GM / Sales) - (Admin Exp / Sales) - (Selling Exp / Sales) - (R&D / Sales) - (Taxes / Sales) - ...
  ```
  * Where `GM (Gross Margin) = Sales - Cost of Sales` (Can be analyzed by product/line of business)
  ```
  Other Items PM = Other Operating Income / Sales
                 = (Subsidiary Income / Sales) + (Equity Dividends / Sales) + (Special Items / Sales) + (Other Net Gain & Losses / Sales) + ...
  ```
  * **Identifying Core OI vs Unusual Items (from Screenshot 13):** Need to separate sustainable operating income from temporary/unusual items (e.g., restructuring charges, asset write-downs) to get **Core Operating Income**. Core OI = Core sales revenue - Core cost of sales - Core operating expenses + Core other operating income - Tax allocated to unusual items. Comprehensive Operating Income = Core OI +/- Unusual Items.

* **Asset Turnover Drivers (from Screenshot 2):**
  ```
  1 / ATO = NOA / Sales
          = (Cash / Sales) + (Acc. Rec. / Sales) + (Inventory / Sales) + ...
          + (PPE / Sales) + (Subsidiary Inv. / Sales) + ...
          - (Payables / Sales) - (Pension Oblig. / Sales) - ...
  ```
  > Note: The inverse of ATO (NOA/Sales) is used so individual components can be aggregated. Lower component ratios mean higher turnover/efficiency for that asset/liability relative to sales.

* **Other Turnover-Related Measures (from Screenshot 2):**
  * `Days in Acc. Receivable = Acc. Receivable / Avg. Sales per day = 365 / Accounts Receivable Turnover`
  * `Inventory Turnover = Cost of Sales / Avg. Inventories`
  * `Acc. Payable Turnover = Purchases / Avg. Acc. Payable`

### 4.4. Analysis of Net Borrowing Cost (NBC) (from Screenshot 3)

NBC is the weighted average cost of net financing.
```
NBC = [ (FO / NFO) * (After-tax Interest on Fin. Oblig. / FO) ]
    - [ (FA / NFO) * (After-tax Interest on Fin. Assets / FA) ]
    - [ (FA / NFO) * (Unrealized Gains on FA / FA) ]
    + [ (Preferred Stock / NFO) * (Pref. Divs. / Preferred Stock) ]
    + ...
```
Where:
* FO = Financial Obligations
* FA = Financial Assets
* NFO = Net Financial Obligations = FO - FA

## 5. Cost of Capital & Required Returns (Related to Step 4)

### 5.1. Cost of Capital for Operations (ρ<sub>F</sub>) (from Screenshot 6)

* Operations have **operational risk** (changes in customer demand, input costs, political uncertainty, etc.).
* This risk determines the required return (cost of capital) to invest in operations, denoted `ρ_F`.
* This is the firm's cost of capital before considering financing effects.
* It's related to the **Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC)**:
  ```
  ρ_F = (V_0^E / V_0^F) * ρ_E + (V_0^D / V_0^F) * ρ_D
  or
  ρF = V₀ᴱ/V₀ᶠ ρE + V₀ᴰ/V₀ᶠ ρD
  ```
  * Uses *market values* (V) rather than book values.
  * `V_0^F = V_0^E + V_0^D` (Total Firm Value = Equity Value + Debt Value)
  * `ρ_E` = Cost of Equity
  * `ρ_D` = After-tax Cost of Debt = Nominal cost of debt * (1 - t)
    * 't' is the marginal tax rate.

### 5.2. Cost of Equity Capital (ρ<sub>E</sub>) (from Screenshot 7)

* Can be derived from the cost of capital for operations (`ρ_F`):
  ```
  ρ_E = ρ_F + (V_0^D / V_0^E) * (ρ_F - ρ_D)
  or
  ρE = V₀ᶠ/V₀ᴱ ρF - V₀ᴰ/V₀ᴱ ρD = ρF + V₀ᴰ/V₀ᴱ (ρF-ρD)
  ```
  > This views operations (`ρ_F`) as the source of risk, adjusted by the risk borne by equity investors due to leverage. `V_0^D / V_0^E` represents market leverage.

* **Equity risk has two components:**
  1. **Operating risk:** Risk inherent in the business operations (captured by `ρ_F`).
  2. **Financing risk:** Additional risk due to leverage (captured by the spread `ρ_F - ρ_D` and the amount of leverage).

### 5.3. Required Return vs. Accounting Return 

* **Required Return on Equity (`ρ_E`):** Driven by `ρ_F` and market leverage (as above).
* **Accounting Return on Equity (ROCE):** Driven by RNOA and *book* leverage.
  ```
  ROCE = RNOA + (NFO / CSE) * (RNOA - NBC)
  ```
  > Leverage increases *both* accounting return (if spread > 0) and required return. High risk -> High (required) return.

## 6. Enterprise Valuation & The Impact of Leverage (Steps 4, 5)

### 6.1. Valuing Operations (Enterprise Valuation)

* **Focus on ReOI:** Because financing activities typically don't add value (assuming fair terms) and P/B or P/E can be distorted by leverage, it's often better to value the operations directly.
  ```
  V_0_Operations = NOA_0 + PV of future ReOI
  ```
  Where `ReOI_t = OI_t - (ρ_F - 1) * NOA_{t-1}`
* **Value of Equity:**
  ```
  V_0_Equity = V_0_Operations - V_0_Net Debt
  ```
  (Value of Operations - Market Value of Net Financial Obligations)
* **Advantages:**
  * Focuses forecasting on operations (OI, NOA).
  * Required return (`ρ_F`) doesn't need adjustment for changing leverage during the forecast.
  * Assumes NFO on the balance sheet is close to market value.

### 6.2. Effect of Leverage on Valuation 

* Leverage changes the *distribution* of value between debt and equity but doesn't create value itself (assuming fair financing costs).
* **Example:** Firm valuation using ReOI with 9% cost of capital for operations (`ρ_F`) and 5% after-tax cost of debt (`ρ_D`).
  * Initial state (e.g., no debt): Calculate ReOI, PV of ReOI, Value of Equity.
  * Leveraged state (e.g., takes on debt to repurchase shares):
    * Cost of Equity (`ρ_E`) increases due to higher leverage: `ρ_E = ρ_F + (NFO / CSE) * (ρ_F - ρ_D)`. (In the example, goes from 9% to 10%).
    * Net Income changes due to interest expense.
    * Book Value (CSE) changes.
    * Residual Earnings (RE, using `ρ_E`) changes.
    * **Total Value of Equity remains the same** if the transaction (debt issuance, share repurchase) occurs at fair value.
    * Value per share *remains the same* if repurchase is at fair value.
    * ROCE increases (due to positive spread).
    * P/B increases (because P stays same, B decreases).
    * P/E decreases (because E (required return) increases, P stays same).

If the 16.7 million shares had been repurchased at $43.33 rather than $36, the comparison is:


* **Do they add value?** Generally NO, if done at fair market value. They just change leverage and redistribute value.
* **Exception:** Repurchasing shares below fair value *can* add value to remaining shareholders (transfers wealth from selling shareholders). Example compares Reebok repurchase at $43.33 vs. $36.
* **Why do managers do it?**
  * Pay out cash (flexible alternative to dividends).
  * Offset dilution from stock options (though value per share isn't truly diluted if options were issued fairly).
  * **Increase EPS/ROCE:** *This does not create value* but may boost managers' compensation tied to these metrics. **Bad practice!**
  * Signal positive future prospects (market timing).

* **The Three Caveats:** Beware of earnings growth created by:
  1. Investment (if return < cost of capital)
  2. Accounting methods
  3. **Financial leverage**
  > None of these necessarily create shareholder value.

### 7.2. Forecasting Template Steps (from Screenshot 17 - *Only steps 1-7 needed for ReOI Valuation*)

1. Forecast **Sales**.
2. Forecast **ATO** and calculate **NOA** (`NOA = Sales / ATO`).
3. Revise Sales forecast for asset constraints if necessary.
4. Forecast **Core PM** and calculate **Core OI from Sales** (`Core OI = Sales * Core PM`).
5. Forecast **Other Core OI** and total **Core OI** (`Core OI = Core OI from Sales + Other Core OI`).
6. Forecast **Unusual Operating Items (UI)**.
7. Calculate **Total OI** (`OI_t = Core OI_t + UI_t`) and **ReOI** (`ReOI_t = OI_t – (ρ_F – 1) * NOA_{t-1}`).
8. *(Optional for DCF: Calculate Free Cash Flow (`C – I = OI – ΔNOA`))*
9. *(Optional for full statements: Forecast net dividend payout (`d`))*
10. *(Optional for full statements: Calculate financial expenses (`NFE_t = (ρ_D – 1) * NFO_{t-1}`))*
11. *(Optional for full statements: Update NFO (`NFO_t = NFE_t + d_t – (C – I)_t`))*
12. *(Optional for full statements: Calculate comprehensive earnings (`Earnings_t = OI_t – NFE_t`))*
13. *(Optional for full statements: Calculate common stockholders’ equity (`CSE_t = NOA_t – NFO_t = CSE_{t-1} + Earnings_t - d_t`))*
